## Background

The International Nucleotide Sequence Database Collaboration (INSDC) — NCBI's SRA, EMBL-EBI's ENA, and DDBJ — holds decades of accumulated experimental biology. Millions of samples across the major model organisms sit in these archives, free for the taking, and they constitute an irreplaceable substrate for meta-analyses, reference atlases, and machine-learning training corpora. The scientific value of the underlying data is not in question; the volume of life-science research that depends on these archives in any given week is enormous.

The utility of that data, however, is throttled at the metadata gate. Sample annotations are deposited as free-text, with predictable consequences: a 2025 crawl of the plant transcriptomics slice alone identified over seventy distinct lexical patterns expressing that a sample is a "control," and the same problem recurs across tissue, treatment, ecotype, and growth-condition fields. The archives are aware of this. The most recent EBI BioSamples update (NAR Database issue, 2026) describes substantial work on the submission side — checklists, controlled vocabularies, validation rules — aimed at improving the quality of *incoming* records.

But intake-side improvements do not reach the legacy. INSDC has no curation mechanism for records already deposited: published metadata is effectively immutable, and the inconsistencies accumulated across decades of varying submission practice remain in place exactly as they were. The practical consequence is that every secondary use of the archive repeats the same harmonisation work in private, with no channel to contribute that work back to a community resource. The data are there; the metadata gate is closed.

OverARC addresses the legacy problem directly. It is a federated curation pattern that overlays the existing archive without modifying it, accumulates community-contributed harmonisation under standard open-source workflow on the nfdi4plants DataHub, and renders the result into deterministic, DOI-citable consensus artifacts. Prior efforts to close the metadata gap have taken either centralised automated approaches (MetaSRA, ESPERANTO, EBI BioSamples) or organism-specific manual curation efforts (ARS for Arabidopsis); none produces shared, evolving, version-controlled curation overlays that the broader community can extend and re-render into citable artifacts.

## Approach

OverARC is a curation pattern, a substrate, a deterministic rendering workflow, and a reference contributor application.

The substrate is a collection of **curation ARCs** — Git-versioned, ISA-compliant FAIR Digital Objects hosted on the nfdi4plants DataHub, one per INSDC BioProject — each carrying three co-equal curatorial layers. ISA assay sheets carry structured per-sample experimental parameters (growth conditions, tissue, developmental stage, treatment) with units and ontology references. SSSOM tables, embedded as DataMaps, encode term-level mappings of free-text annotations to established biomedical ontologies (Plant Ontology, Plant Environment Ontology, NCBI Taxonomy, OBI), with curator identity, confidence, and justification per row. ISA-framework outputs annotated by datamaps capture transformations that exceed SSSOM's term-level expressiveness — for instance, decomposing a free-text title into multiple typed values. Each layer is directly authored or auto-generated; none derives the others.

The **OverARC** application is the reference contributor client. It crawls INSDC for the full record set (study, biosample, experiment, run) of a chosen BioProject and persists it to a local SQLite cache using the typed `BioFSharp.FileFormats.INSDC` library. If no curation ARC exists for that BioProject on the DataHub, OverARC creates one via the `DataHubClient` library and emits a **baseline initial commit**: fields that map 1:1 (e.g. project title → investigation title) populate the ISA structure across all the BioProject's samples, paired with an auto-generated SSSOM file at ~0.8 confidence. The curator works forward from this baseline; refinement, mapping correction, structured-parameter authoring, and datamap-tracked transformations accumulate as further commits.

Contributions follow standard open-source workflow: issues, branches, pull requests, maintainer review under nfdi4plants DataHub group permissions. Continuous integration validates SSSOM schema, predicate validity, ontology term resolvability at pinned versions, and cross-reference integrity against an index of the 2026 INSDC re-crawl. The substrate is client-agnostic: OverARC is one entry point, but any tooling — CLI, Jupyter notebook, third-party web application, direct Git push — can open pull requests against either the curation ARC or its paired consensus ARC. Quality is enforced by code at the substrate level rather than by gatekeeping a privileged write path; this is the structural condition for federation to actually hold rather than to be a façade over a centralised process.

Each curation ARC is paired 1:1 with a **consensus ARC** that is rendered deterministically by a CWL workflow shipped inside the curation ARC itself. The rendering applies an explicit reduction policy (handling multiple mappings of the same input, predicate preference, alternative preservation) and produces a versioned, ISA-structured ARC with full provenance per row back to the source commit. Re-rendering runs automatically on every merge to the curation ARC. DOI minting of a tagged release is a separate, explicit human action. Reproducibility is exact: anyone holding the curation ARC at a given commit can re-render the consensus ARC bit-for-bit. The DOI-tagged consensus ARC is the stable citation target for downstream consumers.

## Demonstration

We exercise the pattern on *Arabidopsis thaliana*. The auto-baseline mechanism scales far beyond what hand curation alone could cover: OverARC generates baseline curation ARCs across a broad slice of Arabidopsis accessions in the 2026 INSDC re-crawl, each populated with auto-mapped ISA and a paired auto-SSSOM file at ~0.8 confidence on the 1:1-resolvable fields. Hand curation under a shared standard operating procedure, performed in double-curated form on a defined subset for inter-rater agreement assessment, accumulates on top.

Two complementary layers of statistics characterise the released collection. **Baseline coverage** measures how far auto-mapping reaches across the Arabidopsis BioProject space: the number of baseline curation ARCs generated, per-field auto-mapping coverage, the distribution of auto-SSSOM confidence, and the ISA-completeness gains attributable to the baseline commit alone. **Hand-curation quality** on the curated subset reports lexical reduction ratio per metadata field (free-text strings collapsed to ontology terms), per-field ontology coverage stacked by SSSOM predicate, inter-curator agreement (Cohen's κ) on the double-curated subset, and the accretion of contributions and contributors over time.

In parallel we present a fresh characterisation of the **2026 INSDC re-crawl** itself — refreshed lexical pattern enumeration across the plant transcriptomics slice (extending the "70+ control patterns" finding to treatment, tissue, ecotype, growth condition, and developmental stage), per-field free-text cardinality and long-tail distribution, cross-field consistency anomalies, and structural characterisation of record relationships across the study/biosample/experiment/run hierarchy. Biological re-analyses of the curated corpus — variance decomposition across context factors and context-matched differential-expression re-analysis — are in preparation as a separate report.

## Contribution

OverARC's contribution is structural. It delivers (i) the federated curation pattern itself, with SSSOM as the term-level mapping format, an ISA-framework + datamap layer for transformations that exceed SSSOM, a deterministic CWL consensus-rendering workflow, and OverARC as the reference contributor client; (ii) a released collection of Arabidopsis per-BioProject curation ARCs — baseline-populated across a broad slice of the 2026 re-crawl, hand-curated on a defined subset — together with their paired DOI-tagged consensus ARCs on the nfdi4plants DataHub, open to community extension; and (iii) statistics characterising the substrate's transformation of INSDC metadata at both layers, alongside the snapshot characterisation of the 2026 re-crawl.

The pattern is generalisable beyond Arabidopsis and beyond INSDC. It addresses the legacy-metadata problem that intake-side improvements, however welcome, cannot reach — bringing decades of deposited research data back to life as a curated, citable, community-extensible resource.
